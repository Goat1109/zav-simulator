# EPIC → SUPER hardware integration

Source: [zav-simulator / ZAVSIM_Fix](https://github.com/ZenithFuturesLab/zav-simulator/tree/ZAVSIM_Fix/src/epic_ros2), commit `36bf6ef5a5a13bb7e002d0f82a5c46953afb2cfa`. Only `epic_ros2` was imported. License and source hashes are in `epic_ros2/LICENSE` and `epic_ros2/UPSTREAM.json`. Native mapping, frontier selection and external guide generation remain upstream code; the external manual reset also accepts LAND so a new, explicit RC7 session is possible.

## Build and operate

Inside `argo_dev`, run `scripts/build_super_hardware.sh`, then source `install/setup.bash`. The web server needs a restart to load its updated Python backend; restarting it does not stop detached ROS jobs.

1. On the web page choose **gym**, **frontier whole**, or **frontier single floor**, then run **EPIC + SUPER 자율탐사** and **SUPER 브리지** while disarmed and landed before takeoff. A stationary ground pose locks the profile alignment; an airborne launch cannot acquire it. The profile is fixed for that launch; stop and relaunch on the ground to change it.
2. For an Offboard ground start leave the base switch in Position, select RC8 HIGH while disarmed and stationary, leave both sticks centered and wait for `SUPER_AUTO_PILOT_ARM` (actual Position). Hold the left stick down and right with the right stick centered until PX4 confirms arming. You may release it to center. The bridge streams local NED position targets. In actual Offboard the PX4 position controller climbs 0.4 m and holds the recorded XY and yaw. With `tracking_backend: px4` (the hardware default in `config/common.yaml`), PX4 keeps position control after takeoff. The bridge sends the sampled SUPER position, velocity feedforward and yaw in LOCAL_NED throughout hover, pilot motion, EPIC, patrol and return. On the external-vision heading profile, the bridge uses the same fixed axis conversion as the external-vision odometry and anchors the translation at Offboard entry. Selecting `ommpc` before launch restores the thrust measurement and body-rate handoff. With RC8 LOW, take off manually in Position and establish a stationary hover. Enter actual Offboard with the existing RC8 procedure. The hover position must be inside the aircraft's WORLD height limits with the 3 cm inset.
   After Offboard hover, center all four sticks to enable manual stick control. Pitch/roll command horizontal movement relative to vehicle heading, throttle commands vertical movement about its midpoint, and yaw commands heading. Releasing the sticks commands a position hold. These manual references do not use SUPER obstacle planning.
3. Keep RC7 LOW or HIGH for at least 0.3 s, then place it in the middle (1400–1600 µs) for at least 0.3 s. It starts only with healthy SUPER Offboard, fresh state/cloud/RC7, fresh airborne confirmation, EPIC WAIT_TRIGGER, and a normal battery above 20%.
   If the hover is outside the exploration boxes or profile height band, SUPER first plans a map-checked approach from the measured position to an interior point. The controller reports `ENTERING_BOUNDS`. After the approach ends and odometry confirms a stopped vehicle inside, the controller sends the EPIC trigger. Keep RC7 in the middle through both phases.
   During manual motion the RC7 edge first requests a stop. EPIC starts once the controller and FC confirm hover. Movement sticks are ignored during exploration.
4. Move RC7 out of the middle to stop exploration and request a current-position hold. Center all sticks before resuming manual stick control. RC8 LOW remains the pilot's existing mode takeover. A failed start, stopped session or recovered link never restarts just because RC7 is still in the middle; move it outside and back in again.

CLI equivalent (start the existing SUPER bridge separately):

```bash
ros2 launch zav_super super_navigation.launch.py execute:=true epic_enabled:=true epic_profile:=gym
# epic_profile:=frontier_whole | frontier_single_floor
```

## Profiles and coordinates

| Web label | Original file | Source viewing Z band | Hardware profile viewing Z band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gym | gym-epic-full-local.yaml | 0.3–1.5 m | 0.3–1.5 m |
| frontier whole | frontier_bounds.yaml | unspecified | 0.3–9.5 m |
| frontier single floor | frontier-first-floor-sim.yaml | 1.2–1.9 m | 1.2–1.9 m |

The three profile YAML files retain their selected boundary geometry. Their boundary geometry is aligned to the robot's initial ground position and heading, as requested. Gym and frontier whole use profile reference `(0,0,0)`, yaw 0°. Frontier single-floor hardware anchors the launch position at `(-3.26924,-0.05305,0.18920)` and the initial nose at yaw −180° (profile −X). Place the nose toward the connector, perpendicular to the long corridor (profile Y).

`epic_frame_adapter` captures one stationary, disarmed, confirmed-ground pose with a fresh FC heartbeat. It applies a rigid yaw/translation transform to odometry, world velocity and registered cloud XYZ before EPIC, then the inverse to EPIC guides before SUPER. Body angular velocity and sensor mounting stay unchanged. The controller checks guides and local trajectories in that same fixed profile frame, plus SUPER's physical world-height limits. It also watches the transform heartbeat: a missing/changed transform stops exploration, and a changed transform requires a ground relaunch. The anchor never resets just because the pilot leaves Offboard or disarms.

For first floor the current YAML viewing band is profile Z=0.8–1.7 m, before intersection with hardware limits. Its height relative to the initial ground pose is approximately 0.61–1.51 m because the reference Z is 0.18920 m. Actual allowable flight heights depend on the measured ground anchor.

SUPER's current common virtual ceiling is 1.7 m and optimizer speed is 1 m/s. The EPIC session's hard WORLD limits remain common ground +0.2 m and ceiling −0.5 m (currently −0.4–1.2 m). YAML flight heights must never be numerically clipped against these WORLD values before anchoring. With profile translation `tz`, native EPIC intersects its requested profile band with `[world_floor+tz+0.03, world_ceiling+tz−0.03]`. An empty intersection blocks RC7 start. SUPER also clips all exploration/backup/empty safety corridors to the same WORLD limits, inset by 3 cm; polynomial certification retains the original hard limits. The controller converts hard WORLD planes to PROFILE exactly once, preserves the union of XY boundary boxes, and reports both limits and the aligned band in `/zav/epic/status`.

## Data flow and FSM

`/argo/super/{odom,cloud_world}` → fixed profile-frame adapter → `/epic/profile_{odom,cloud}` → EPIC external planner → `/epic/profile_path` → inverse frame adapter → `/mission/exploration_path` → hardware controller → `/zav/super/goal` → SUPER polynomial trajectory → OMMPC → existing single MAVLink bridge.

The EPIC launch starts `exploration_node` and its TSP worker; the hardware integration also starts the coordinate adapter; no simulator, trajectory server, command adapter, FC link, arm, takeoff, or landing command is started. EPIC's unused takeoff/land output is isolated on `/epic/unused_takeoff_land`. EPIC state markers are remapped to `/epic/state`. Raw `/livox/imu` timestamps are the independent freshness reference. Planning runs at nice 10 with two OpenMP threads.

The controller publishes `/zav/epic/status` at 10 Hz: native FSM, profile, start/stop reason, blocked condition, RC7 freshness/edge readiness and current guide leg. The web subscriber marks this stale independently of FC telemetry. Controller status identifies the mission as `epic`, accepted by the existing bridge schema.

The controller uses the simulator's ordered 3D polyline projection and interpolated forward target. Preview starts at 4 m, grows with braking distance (acceleration 6 m/s², jerk 10 m/s³), and is capped at 15 m. A 0.15 m chord-error limit preserves corners/stairs, and reversals cannot be bypassed before arrival. Projection searches only a forward arc window, so crossings/return overlaps cannot globally snap progress to a later leg. Identical previews preserve progress; a changed guide is validated and adopted while moving.

Goals update at most 5 Hz, after >0.3 m target movement, >0.1 rad yaw change, route revision or first final preview. At low speed an unresolved target may be retried after 1 s. There is exactly one outstanding request: newer desired targets wait for its response without resetting its identity/deadline. The committed trajectory remains active while waiting. Cached SUPER packets retain their original request identity and accepted goal until their polynomial content changes. Only a fresh, correctly correlated, in-bounds response replaces it; the accepted map-adjusted goal must stay within the configured 0.3 m snap limit. New goals clear terminal settling. Intermediate vertices do not require stopping or a 1 s hold. Final arrival retains the accepted-goal 3D distance <0.1 m and 3D speed <0.1 m/s, plus the committed terminal-stop check; the extra EPIC arrival dwell is removed. Patrol/takeoff/RTH dwell policies remain separate.

Native FSM status has a separate callback-group timer and publishes a mutex-protected snapshot during a long planning callback. It never reads planner/odometry data concurrently. A main-loop snapshot older than 5 s is reported as CAUTION and stops exploration; whole-process silence still trips the 1.5 s controller watchdog. The native 5 s no-guide deadline remains independent. Hardware planning loops explicitly use two OpenMP workers.

## Failure behavior

- Profile-transform loss (0.6 s), a changed anchor, RC7 loss (0.5 s), non-middle PWM, unhealthy control/feedback, missing airborne confirmation, low/unknown battery, or measured boundary exit ends the EPIC session. EPIC's manual-reset topic is asserted, cached routes are cleared, the SUPER request is cancelled on `/zav/super/planner_cancel`, and an EPIC mission is replaced by a current-position OMMPC hold request within the existing configured rate/thrust limits. In-flight optimization may finish, but its result is suppressed and its old goal is not repeatedly replanned. A later, newer goal can resume the planner. Hold is possible only while the existing controller and feedback remain healthy.
- EPIC state silence beyond 1.5 s, native CAUTION/LAND, or 5 s without a new guide while waiting stops the session. FINISH/completion holds; it does not automatically land. EPIC may supply its native checked return guide before completion; it is subject to the same bounds checks.
- Guide checks reject stale/replayed/pre-session messages, wrong frame, nonfinite coordinates, invalid quaternion, oversized path, disconnected departure, long segments and segments crossing gaps between boundary boxes. An invalid live guide ends the session. During exploration each SUPER polynomial is certified against the exploration bounds using its Bezier convex hull and bounded subdivision, including excursions between time samples. Neighboring raw guide vertices retain the 2 m packet limit; rolling preview chords use separate full-union containment checks.
- Existing SUPER heartbeat, plan deadline, solver, odometry reset/jump, tracking and EV/FC failsafes remain in force. With healthy EV/FC feedback, a latched controller fault immediately replaces body-rate setpoints with a captured PX4 local-position/yaw hold and requests Position on the existing UART. It continues the stabilizing stream until the FC heartbeat confirms mode exit. If Position remains unconfirmed for 2 s, it requests PX4 AUTO.LAND instead. Any pilot mode exit ends this recovery; it never resumes rates automatically. EV-loss, pose reset/jump and the existing EV LAND owner take priority and disable this position recovery. `/zav_real/status.offboard.fault_recovery` records the handoff stage. The UI reports actual FC mode independently.
- Forward/Patrol commands are blocked during EPIC. Explicit RTH can preempt it, then follows the existing Home/landing rules. Web disconnect does not terminate flight nodes.

The bounds check is a command admission/monitoring guard, not a proof that the aircraft can brake before every boundary or obstacle. Physical flight validation and measured stopping clearance remain necessary.

## Validation

`test/epic_gate.cpp` checks switch edges, debounce, loss/recovery, rejected starts and exact segment containment in a union of boxes. `test/epic_pipeline.py` exercises the ROS controller contract using synthetic inputs in a network-disabled container without serial devices. Frame unit tests cover first-floor alignment, inverse guide/odometry transforms, world velocity, body angular velocity and padded little/big-endian point clouds. The ROS contract test also runs through the real frame adapter, including refusal to anchor in flight and transform loss. Native EPIC tests and existing SUPER/web tests are also run. An additional device-free test runs the actual EPIC node with the actual profile adapter on a synthetic room, using a 90° frame rotation and translation, and verifies that its generated guide returns to hardware world coordinates. These tests do not establish real-aircraft safety or a full PX4 flight simulation.

## EPIC debugging bag

Choose **epic** in the web Bag selector, or run `scripts/run_bag.sh epic` in the ROS container (`bag_epic` in a new container shell). It writes `bags/epic_debug_YYMMDD_HHMMSS/`. All existing superlivo raw sensor topics (LiDAR, IMU, camera) are included, together with EPIC FSM/trigger/manual/completion, profile/world guides and transform, SUPER goal/polynomial/controller/rates, bridge/FC status, LIO/EPIC odometry, time cost and rosout. A rejected polynomial sends request/start-time-correlated `/zav/super/trajectory_feedback`. SUPER discards that cached response and generates a fresh plan from measured position/velocity, retaining the same goal identity. Feedback neither restarts the controller's 1 s replacement deadline nor extends old references. An unchanged rolling goal is retried only after the committed trajectory has ended in a certified stop short of its accepted goal; low speed during final braking or arrival does not create a new request. Duplicate/stale feedback is ignored.

Extra diagnostics include `/zav/super/trajectory_feedback`, `/epic/decision`, `/epic/heartbeat`, `/zav/super/planner_cancel`, `/zav/super/planner_status`, `/planning/pos_cmd`, `/super_lio/processing_time`, `/tf`, `/tf_static` and `/parameter_events`. The upstream LIO processing-time hook may be silent in this version; absent topics remain discoverable by the recorder. IMU is listed only once.

QoS overrides accept best-effort sensors and SUPER outputs; retained EPIC status, profile path/transform, decision, completion, cancellation and static TF use reliable transient-local subscriptions so starting a bag during exploration captures them. Registered/profile clouds are not duplicated: the raw LiDAR is already included. `/zav/epic/status` records route revision/progress/preview length, request identity/age and committed request identity, allowing a stalled request to be distinguished from a rejected response or guide update. `/zav/super/planner_status` journals accepted/rejected requests, committed trajectories and cancellation acknowledgements with request identities. Recording does not launch or stop flight nodes.

### Remaining validation limits

The real-planner regression uses a compact synthetic room and ideal scripted odometry, including 2 m and 1 m forward legs and an approximately 3 m RTH. During development, a longer approximately 9 m RTH in the synthetic room repeatedly failed the existing position/omega/thrust optimization checks and reached the unchanged 1 s replacement deadline. That longer case is not claimed as validated; optimizer tuning and the deadline were not relaxed. The continuous-guide test separately completed a 6 m route with eight goal updates while moving. These contract checks do not establish vehicle dynamics, real obstacle avoidance, stopping clearance, CPU margin with full-rate camera/LIO/recording, or resolution of real-flight vibration.
